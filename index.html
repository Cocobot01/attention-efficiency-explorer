Beyond Quadratic

Standard self-attention compares every query position with every key position, so its cost grows with the square of sequence length. This explorer walks through four attention variants and separates the ones that genuinely reduce that cost from the one that only appears to.

The four variants

Standard (full) attention — every position attends to every other. Quadratic in time and memory, and the reference point for accuracy.

Naive sliding-window (masked) — builds the full score matrix, then masks it down to a local window. It reproduces the accuracy of a local method without any of the saving: the expensive step still happens, so it lands slightly slower than standard attention.

Disjoint block — the sequence is cut into non-overlapping blocks and each position attends only inside its own block. Genuinely linear, but information never crosses a block boundary, not even into the adjacent block.

Overlapping sliding-window — each block additionally attends to its immediate left and right neighbours, in the manner of Longformer. Still linear, with roughly three times the scores per query and a receptive field that widens with depth.

Panel 03 · Capability

Task accuracy

Task:
The model must reproduce a sequence of length 128 presented earlier in the input, a copy distance of about 129 tokens. Success requires carrying information across that gap.
Two results, read separately. At every window size a long sequence would actually use, both variants sit at chance: a local window cannot route information across a gap wider than its reach. At w=128 the overlapping variant reaches 0.99, but only three blocks exist at that setting, so every block sees every other and this is effectively full attention. It is evidence that the overlap mechanism works — the disjoint variant, which never crosses a block boundary, stays at chance in the identical configuration — and not evidence that local attention solves long-range tasks.

Depth study

Stacking local-attention layers widens the receptive field in theory, since each layer carries information one window further. This panel asks whether that recovers the copy task in practice.

The control is what makes this readable. Standard attention holds high accuracy at every depth, so the residual connections, learning rate and training loop are sound at depth; a flat local-attention curve therefore reflects the architecture rather than a broken setup. The claim stays bounded: depth did not compensate within the range tested. Four layers at w=32 gives a theoretical reach well short of the copy distance, so this does not show that depth could never help.
